The lab investigates electronic and electrochemical interfacial phenomena to build intelligent hardware. Our research is structured across five integrated pillars, spanning fundamental charge transport to functional microelectronic chips and wearable devices.

RESEARCH PILLAR 01

Neuromorphic Computing & Bio-Realistic Synaptic Transistors

Conventional von Neumann digital hardware suffers severe energy bottlenecks when running deep neural networks. We design and fabricate fully screen-printed organic electrochemical transistors (OECTs) that emulate biological synapses with ultra-low operating energy (~24 nJ).

  • Biological Memory Curves: Successful hardware replication of the psychological Ebbinghaus forgetting curve.
  • Plasticity Rules: Spike-Timing-Dependent Plasticity (STDP) and Paired-Pulse Facilitation (PPF).
  • Integrated Logic: Demonstrating concurrent multi-input OR logic inside the conductive channel.
OECT Transistors Hebbian Learning Edge AI Hardware Applied Materials Today 2026
RESEARCH PILLAR 02

Organic Electrochemical Transistors (OECTs) & Biosensors

OECTs operate through volumetric ion penetration into a conjugated polymer channel (e.g., PEDOT:PSS), yielding extraordinary transconductance (gm) orders of magnitude greater than conventional field-effect transistors. We exploit this amplification to detect biological and environmental analytes at sub-nanomolar thresholds.

  • Water Contaminant Sensors: Protein-immobilized gates for ultrasensitive detection of phenolic and aromatic water pollutants.
  • Genomic Recognition: OECT platforms specifically sensitive to DNA-binding protein complexes.
  • Granted Patents: Protected under Indian Patent No. 474656 and Patent No. 431170.

RESEARCH PILLAR 03

Sustainable Printed Electronics & Green Graphene Synthesis

Industrial microelectronics manufacturing faces pressing environmental sustainability challenges. We developed a rapid, chemical-free microwave-assisted route to synthesize graphene flakes directly from natural graphite flakes.

  • Sub-2V Operating Transistors: Photo-thermally reduced graphene oxide (rGO) gated via high-capacitance room-temperature ion-gel dielectrics.
  • Green Microwave Synthesis: Microwave-assisted transformation minimizing toxic acid oxidizers (Patent No. 596620).
  • International Collaboration: Developed in synergy with the Indo-German Science & Technology Centre (IGSTC) and Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT).
Carbon 2025 (IF: 11.6) Indian Patent 586571 Green Microwave Synthesis KIT Germany IGSTC
RESEARCH PILLAR 04

Piezoresistive Strain Sensors & Smart Biomedical Sponges

Porous conductive elastomer composites provide unprecedented dynamic range for human biomechanical and physiological signal extraction. We engineer 3D reduced graphene oxide-carbon nanotube-melamine foam networks for highly compressible transducers.

  • Piezoresistive Microstructures: Porous 3D conductive skeletons exhibiting linear resistance modulation over large mechanical deformation regimes.
  • Biomedical Monitoring: High-fidelity tracking of arterial pulse waveforms, vocal cord vibrations, and acoustic speech recognition using edge ML.
  • Mechanical Robustness: Enduring thousands of loading/unloading cycles without hysteresis fatigue.

RESEARCH PILLAR 05

Screen-Printable Sustainable Graphene Inks & Flexible Wearables

Translating green 2D materials into industrial printed electronics requires precise control over dispersion stability and shear-thinning rheology. We combine sustainable microwave-assisted graphene synthesis with tailored ink formulation for high-resolution screen-printed flexible wearable devices.

  • Green Microwave Synthesis: Rapid, low-toxicity exfoliation of graphene flakes suited for scalable ink production.
  • Rheological Engineering: Shear-thinning, thixotropic ink behavior optimized for crisp screen-printed conductive traces on flexible substrates.
  • Flexible Wearable Devices: High conductivity and mechanical durability under repeated bending for wearable bioelectronic sensing.
